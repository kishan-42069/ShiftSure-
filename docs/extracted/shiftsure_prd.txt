1. Executive Summary
ShiftSure is a workforce decision-support platform designed to help organizations redeploy employees when automation, AI
adoption, or operational disruptions reduce the amount of available work. Instead of immediately sending workers home,
reducing overtime opportunities, or recruiting externally, ShiftSure identifies available internal capacity, matches workers to
suitable open tasks, evaluates short training bridges, and measures fairness and economic implications.
The platform combines deterministic business rules, mathematical optimization, workforce data, fairness analytics, and an
explanation layer. Managers retain control over every final decision.
Prototype boundaries
 One simulated plant with two to three production lines and approximately 50–100 simulated workers.
 
 Configurable disruption scenarios and idle-hours calculation.
 
 Actual constraint-based workforce optimization, not random assignments.
 
 Internal redeployment, training recommendations, and internal-versus-external hiring comparisons.
 
 Fairness metrics, counterfactual testing, manager approval, worker-facing views, and audit history.
 
 Local execution; cloud deployment, live SAP integration, and production infrastructure are not mandatory.
 
2. Problem Statement
When automation or disruption reduces the workload on a production line, managers must decide whether to redeploy
workers, provide training, reduce overtime, or recruit externally. Decisions made under time pressure and with incomplete
information can leave internal capacity unused, distribute lost work unevenly, overlook training opportunities, or create safety
and transparency risks.
Problems ShiftSure addresses
 Underutilized capacity: employees have available hours while other teams have unfilled work.
 
 Unequal disruption: idle hours, lost overtime, and training access may be distributed unevenly.
 
 Missed internal opportunities: potentially suitable employees may be overlooked in favor of external recruitment.
 
 Safety and compliance risk: assignments may violate certification, shift, availability, or location rules.
 
 Insufficient transparency: workers and managers may not know why an assignment was recommended or rejected.

6. Functional Requirements
6.1 Workforce and task data
 FR-001: Worker records include unique ID, role, line/site, shift, available hours, skills, proficiency, certification status,
 employment category, and relevant overtime data.
 FR-002: Task records include ID, destination, demand/headcount, required skills, mandatory certifications, shift, hours, and
 training/cost assumptions.
 FR-003: Reject or flag duplicate IDs, negative hours, invalid dates, missing mandatory fields, and impossible requirements.
 
 FR-004: Clearly label seeded data and generated results as simulated in the UI and exports.
 
 FR-005: Workers can submit skill corrections; changes remain pending until authorized review. Approved changes are
 auditable and can trigger re-planning.
6.2 Disruption scenarios
 FR-006: Managers can create or configure an automation or operational disruption.
 
 FR-007: Scenario parameters include affected lines, workload reduction or remaining workload, planning period, and optional
 duration.
 FR-008: Calculate idle capacity using workload, existing commitments, and planning-period assumptions; do not treat an
 entire shift as idle automatically.

 FR-009: Scenario execution must not silently alter base workforce data or existing approved plans.
 
6.3 Reallocation optimizer
 FR-010: Use Google OR-Tools or an equivalent real constraint solver against actual records.
 
 FR-011: Enforce certification, skill eligibility, shift, availability, location, capacity, non-overlap, and task-demand rules.
 
 FR-012: Support configurable objectives for coverage, idle hours, training burden, external hiring, and fairness.
 
 FR-013: Persist solver status, assignments, unfilled demand, objective settings, and relevant inputs.
 
 FR-014: Explicitly report infeasible, timed-out, feasible, or optimal outcomes; never fabricate assignments.
 
 FR-015: Use versioned inputs and document solver behavior where multiple equivalent solutions exist.
 
6.4 Three-way comparison
 FR-016: Compare (1) a documented deterministic supervisor-style baseline, (2) optimization without fairness balancing, and
 (3) fairness-balanced optimization.
 FR-017: Evaluate all plans using consistent definitions: idle hours, task coverage, internal fill, unfilled demand, training
 cost/time, hiring cost/lead time, overtime loss where available, and fairness metrics.
 FR-018: Explain trade-offs; do not claim one plan is universally best regardless of priorities.
 
6.5 Training and hiring
 FR-019: Identify workers who qualify now or may qualify after a permitted training bridge.
 
 FR-020: Show missing skills, training duration, estimated cost, and remaining qualification requirements.
 
 FR-021: Compare internal training with configurable external recruitment cost and lead-time estimates.
 
 FR-022: Present options for human review; do not initiate hiring or promise qualification.
 
6.6 Fairness and counterfactual testing
 FR-023: Calculate idle hours, overtime loss where data exists, training access, and relevant redeployment opportunities.
 
 FR-024: Compare configured groups such as contract/permanent and shifts; use demographic data only when appropriate
 and available.
 FR-025: Make review thresholds configurable and identify them as policy settings.
 
 FR-026: Flag disparities for review, not as proof of discrimination.
 
 FR-027: Fairness objectives cannot override hard safety, certification, or eligibility constraints.
 
 FR-028: Run a counterfactual changing one non-job attribute while holding job-relevant factors constant.
 
 FR-029: Show assignment changes, affected workers/tasks, metric differences, and changed/unchanged assumptions.
 
 FR-030: Use equivalent solver settings and account for tie-breaking among equally optimal solutions.
 
 FR-031: Treat changes as review signals, not conclusive proof of unfairness; unchanged outcomes do not prove fairness.
 
6.7 Explanations, approvals, and audit
 FR-032: Explain recommendations from recorded attributes, task requirements, rules, and solver evidence.
 
 FR-033: LLMs may verbalize structured evidence but may not calculate authoritative metrics, invent facts, change results,
 override rules, or approve plans.
 FR-034: Provide deterministic template-based explanations if the LLM is unavailable or returns invalid output.
 
 FR-035: Each explanation should reference the supporting assignment, rule, constraint, or calculation.
 
 FR-036: Managers can review assignments, unfilled tasks, training, costs, fairness flags, and solver status.
 
 FR-037: Support Draft, Generated, Pending Approval, Approved, Rejected, and Superseded states with valid transitions.
 
 FR-038: Only authorized managers can approve/reject; rejection requires a recorded reason.
 
 FR-039: Submitted plans preserve reviewed inputs/results; material changes require a new version or renewed review.
 
 FR-040: Audit scenario changes, solver runs, correction requests, rule changes, and approval decisions.

10. Optimization Model
Decision variables
Let x■■ represent the hours assigned to worker i for task j. Binary assignment variables may be added for tasks requiring
discrete worker selection.
Hard constraints
Worker capacity: Σ■ x■■ ≤ H■, where H■ is available capacity in the planning period.
Task demand: Σ■ x■■ ≤ D■, where D■ is task demand. Minimum required coverage can be modeled separately.
 Ineligible worker-task combinations receive no assignment.
 
 Mandatory certifications must be valid for the task.
 
 Shift, availability, location, capacity, and non-overlap rules must be enforced.
 
 Training only changes eligibility where task rules explicitly permit qualification after training.
 
Objective strategy
A conceptual configurable objective is:
Maximize: w■C − w■I − w■T − w■H − w■F
C = covered demand; I = avoidable idle hours; T = training burden; H = external hiring burden; F = configured fairness
penalty; w values = documented weights. This is a conceptual model, not a mandate to combine every metric into an
uncalibrated score. Avoid double-counting equivalent costs. Safety and mandatory eligibility remain hard constraints.
Solver validation
 Validate certifications, capacity, shifts, overlaps, task eligibility, demand, and training pathways independently.
 
 Reject plans that fail validation.

15. Evaluation and Success Metrics
Operational metrics
Idle hours avoided = baseline idle hours − plan idle hours.
Task coverage = covered demand ÷ total required demand × 100. Define demand consistently in hours, units, or task slots.
Internal fill rate measures the share of covered demand or filled positions satisfied by existing employees. State the
denominator. Compare training and hiring estimates using explicit costs, lead times, and assumptions.
Fairness and quality
 Maximum group-level difference in average idle hours.
 
 Overtime-loss differences when baseline data exists.
 
 Training-offer-rate differences among eligible workers.
 
 Assignment sensitivity in counterfactual testing.
 
 Hard-constraint violations in accepted plans: zero.
 
 Recommendations with traceable explanations.
 
 Correct rejection of invalid inputs and unauthorized approval attempts.
 
 Successful completion of core end-to-end tests.

21. Final Definition of Done
ShiftSure is complete as a prototype when it can take a simulated automation disruption, calculate workforce impact, produce
and validate alternative reallocation plans, compare internal training with external hiring, measure fairness, explain
recommendations, support worker corrections, and record an authorized manager's decision.
A locally runnable, fully functional decision-support application with real optimization,
deterministic metrics, human approval, persistent history, test coverage, and a repeatable
demonstration using clearly labelled simulated data.
Deployment and live enterprise integration are deliberately excluded from the definition of done.
